MAGNETIC FIELD (NOAA WMM)
Measures Earth's magnetic field strength (in nanoTesla) across a grid around the target. Buried stone, brick, and fired clay distort the local magnetic field and create readable patterns.
GOOD SIGNAL Magnetic gradient over 50 nT across the grid = possible buried structures. Sharp gradient over a small area = likely man-made. Flat uniform field = nothing buried.
SAR BACKSCATTER (SENTINEL-1)
Radar reflectivity (in dB) from Sentinel-1. Radar penetrates clouds and detects surface roughness changes caused by buried features pushing up through the soil.
GOOD SIGNAL VV/VH polarization ratio spikes = surface disruption from buried structures. Consistent backscatter anomaly across multiple dates = permanent subsurface feature.
SOLAR RADIATION (NASA POWER)
Surface solar irradiance (in W/m²) from NASA POWER. High sunlight heats the ground more, which can amplify or mask thermal signals from buried structures.
GOOD SIGNAL Stable, consistent irradiance = clean thermal baseline for detecting anomalies. Sudden drops = cloud cover skewing readings. High values = stronger thermal contrast potential.
MAGNETIC FFT SPECTRUM
Breaks the magnetic field into spatial frequencies using FFT. Periodic buried features — evenly spaced walls, roads, ditches — show up as repeating frequency peaks.
GOOD SIGNAL Clean, sharp frequency peaks at regular intervals = periodic buried structures (walls, roads). High magnitude peak with clear period = man-made. Flat spectrum = uniform geology.
MAGNETIC INTENSITY MAP
A 2D heatmap of magnetic field intensity across the scan grid from NOAA's World Magnetic Model. Bright spots show where the field is strongest — where buried materials concentrate.
GOOD SIGNAL Focused bright hotspot = likely buried metallic or fired-clay object. Spread-out gradient = natural geological variation. Uniform color = no subsurface features.
